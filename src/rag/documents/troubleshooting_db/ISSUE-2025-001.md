# ISSUE-2025-001: Weld Porosity on Underbody — Station WLD-01-UBD

**Date:** 2025-03-15 | **Severity:** Major | **Status:** Resolved  
**Affected Models:** PE-SD100, PE-SV200 | **Units Affected:** 47

## Symptom
Destructive peel tests on weld coupons showed porosity (gas pockets) in 12% of underbody spot welds during routine 4-hour quality audit. Normal rate is <2%.

## Investigation
1. Checked weld parameters — current, time, force all within spec
2. Electrode tip condition — dressed, within wear limits
3. Material review — new lot of floor pan steel (SUP-MTL01, Lot SW-2025-0312)
4. Incoming inspection report showed lot passed receiving inspection
5. **Root cause found**: New steel lot had higher zinc coating weight (120 g/m² vs spec 80-100 g/m²). Excess zinc off-gassing during welding created porosity.

## Root Cause
Supplier SteelWorks Inc. (SUP-MTL01) shipped steel with zinc coating weight 20% above specification. Receiving inspection measured thickness but not coating weight specifically.

## Corrective Actions
1. Rejected remainder of lot SW-2025-0312 (NCR-MTL01-00008 issued)
2. Extended squeeze time from 8 to 10 cycles to allow gas escape before weld current
3. Added coating weight measurement to receiving inspection procedure for all zinc-coated steel

## Preventive Actions
1. Updated receiving inspection SOP to include zinc coating weight measurement (X-ray fluorescence)
2. Added coating weight to supplier incoming material specification
3. Monthly supplier scorecard now tracks coating weight compliance

## Lessons Learned
- Dimensional pass at receiving does not guarantee material quality for welding
- Zinc coating weight variation is a known risk for spot welding — must be controlled
- Similar issues documented in automotive industry literature (AWS D8.1M)
