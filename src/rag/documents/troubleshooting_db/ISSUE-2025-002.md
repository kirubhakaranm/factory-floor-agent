# ISSUE-2025-002: Paint Orange Peel Defect Spike — Station PNT-02-PRM

**Date:** 2025-04-22 | **Severity:** Major | **Status:** Resolved  
**Affected Models:** All | **Units Affected:** 31 (1 shift)

## Symptom
Visual inspection at PNT-03-CLR gate reported orange peel defects on 31 of 78 vehicles during Day shift. Normal defect rate is <5%. All colors affected equally.

## Investigation
1. Paint viscosity — checked at mixing room: within spec (22 sec DIN cup #4)
2. Bell atomizer speed — verified at 45,000 rpm: correct
3. Booth temperature — **found at 27°C** (spec: 21-25°C). HVAC system logged temperature rise starting at 09:15 AM
4. Booth humidity — 58% RH (within spec 55-75%)
5. Robot spray parameters — unchanged from previous shift

## Root Cause
Paint booth cooling unit (HVAC chiller) compressor tripped on high-pressure alarm at 09:10 AM. Booth temperature rose from 23°C to 27°C over 90 minutes. Higher temperature caused faster solvent evaporation from paint, resulting in poor flow-out and orange peel texture.

HVAC alarm was logged on BMS but not relayed to paint shop operator HMI — operator was unaware of temperature excursion.

## Corrective Actions
1. Repaired HVAC chiller — replaced high-pressure switch (faulty, reading 15% high)
2. Reworked 31 affected vehicles — wet sand 2000 grit and re-clearcoat
3. Rework cost: approximately $18,600 ($600/vehicle)

## Preventive Actions
1. Added booth temperature alarm to paint shop operator HMI (audible + visual alert if >25°C)
2. Auto-pause production if booth temperature exceeds 26°C for >5 minutes
3. Added redundant temperature sensor as cross-check
4. HVAC PM schedule increased from quarterly to monthly compressor inspection

## Lessons Learned
- Booth temperature is one of the most critical paint quality parameters
- Alarm routing to operators must be verified during HVAC maintenance
- The 2°C temperature rise that caused the defect was subtle — operators may not notice without instrumentation
