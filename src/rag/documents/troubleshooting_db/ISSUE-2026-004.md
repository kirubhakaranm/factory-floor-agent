# ISSUE-2026-004: Recurring Solenoid Valve Overstrain — STP-01-PRS-HYP01

**Date:** 2026-06-25 | **Severity:** Critical | **Status:** Resolved (monitoring)
**Machine:** STP-01-PRS-HYP01 (Schuler TwinServo 2800 Hydraulic Press)
**Station:** STP-01-PRS (Press 1, Stamping Stage)
**Failure ID:** FLR-STP01-2606-007 | **Failure Type:** OSF (Overstrain Failure)
**Component:** Solenoid valve (Moog D661 coil assembly, axis 2)
**Downtime:** 139 minutes | **Units Lost:** 27 | **Total Cost:** €9,943.52

## Symptom

Critical overstrain alarm triggered at 20:00 on Jun 25, 2026. Press slide stopped mid-cycle; HMI showed "Solenoid axis-2 overcurrent — cycle inhibited." Operator reported no unusual sounds or smells prior to shutdown. Machine had been running normally on swing shift.

This was the **third solenoid valve failure in 30 days** on the same machine:
- May 26: PWF (Power failure — solenoid coil burnout, coil replaced)
- Jun 1: TWF (Tool wear — spool scoring found during corrective PM, valve cleaned)
- Jun 25: OSF (Overstrain — coil replaced again under emergency WO-2606-00017)

## Investigation

1. **Pressure review** — System pressure at 211 bar mean (min 191.7, max 237.1 bar over 7 days). Well within the 280 bar max and below the 265 bar alarm threshold. Acute pressure surge ruled out as primary cause.

2. **Maintenance history review** — Three corrective PMs in 6 weeks (May 31, Jun 1, Jun 16). Machine condition restored to 8–9 after each PM but dropped to 3 by Jun 25 — a 9-day degradation cycle that is abnormally fast.

3. **Oil sample (Jun 16 PM)** — Oil sample from Jun 16 PM showed ISO 4406 particle count at 19/17/14 — one class above the servo valve cleanliness requirement of 18/16/13. Marginal contamination present but not flagged as critical.

4. **Coil measurement at teardown** — Failed coil resistance measured at 31 Ω (spec: 26 Ω ±5%, i.e., 24.7–27.3 Ω). High resistance indicates partial insulation breakdown. Coil was running at higher voltage drop → reduced spool actuation force → increased cycle time → further thermal stress.

5. **Replacement coil specification check** — Parts record for May 26 repair showed coil replaced with SP-HYP-007 (correct genuine Moog part). Jun 1 repair used same stock. Specification not the issue.

6. **Cycle frequency review** — Production records showed press cycle rate increased from 8 strokes/min to 11 strokes/min beginning May 20 (production target increase for PE-SV200 batch). This 37% increase in cycling frequency raised solenoid duty cycle beyond the continuous design threshold at ambient temperatures exceeding 35°C during the June heat period.

## Root Cause

**Primary:** Increased production cycle rate (8→11 strokes/min from May 20) combined with summer ambient temperatures (35–38°C measured at press enclosure) pushed solenoid coil thermal load beyond rated continuous duty limits. ISO VG 46 oil viscosity at elevated temperature reduces lubrication film on spool, increasing mechanical friction and coil current draw.

**Contributing:** Marginal oil cleanliness (ISO 19/17/14 vs. required 18/16/13) increased spool stiction, compounding the thermal overstrain. Each corrective PM restored condition but the underlying root causes (high cycle rate + heat + marginal oil) remained, causing rapid re-degradation.

## Corrective Actions

1. Replaced failed solenoid coil with SP-HYP-007 (Jun 25, TECH-009, 139 min).
2. Initiated offline kidney-loop filtration for 8 hours to restore oil cleanliness to ISO 18/16/13.
3. Temporarily reduced press cycle rate to 9 strokes/min pending engineering review of rated operating limits at elevated ambient temperature.

## Preventive Actions

1. **PM interval tightened** — STP-01-PRS-HYP01 moved to 2-week PM cycle (from 3-week) with mandatory solenoid coil resistance check at every PM. If resistance >27.3 Ω, replace coil immediately (do not wait for next PM).

2. **Cycle rate engineering review** — Submitted to process engineering: request formal thermal analysis for TwinServo 2800 at 11 strokes/min and ambient >32°C. Until analysis complete, limit to 9 strokes/min during periods when press enclosure temperature exceeds 30°C.

3. **Oil cleanliness monitoring** — Added ISO particle count check to every 500-hour PM (was every 1,000 hours). Target: maintain ISO ≤18/16/13 at servo valve inlet.

4. **Coil temperature monitoring** — Added infrared thermometer scan of all four solenoid coils to each shift changeover inspection checklist. Alert threshold: coil surface >60°C.

5. **Supplier NCR consideration** — If this solenoid fails again within 30 days, initiate NCR with solenoid coil supplier regardless of specification compliance — batch quality issue cannot be ruled out without 3 consecutive clean cycles.

## Lessons Learned

- Production rate increases must be reviewed against hydraulic component duty cycle limits, not just press force capacity. Solenoid thermal limits are often the binding constraint at high cycle rates.
- Marginal oil cleanliness compounds solenoid wear — servo valve filter cleanliness requirements (ISO 18/16/13) must be maintained, not treated as aspirational.
- Recurring failures of the same component across different failure mode codes (PWF, TWF, OSF) on the same machine within weeks indicate a systemic root cause not addressed by component replacement alone.
- Ambient temperature is a hidden variable in solenoid MTBF. Summer operation requires reduced cycle rate or active enclosure cooling.
